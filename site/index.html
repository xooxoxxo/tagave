What it does

Identifies to the pressing

Matches each album against MusicBrainz and Discogs, with acoustic fingerprints when tags are wrong. It finds your edition and its catalogue number.

Reversible tag correction

Changes come as a plan you preview file by file. Every write is journalled and can be reverted. Files are never moved, renamed or converted.

Gaps and ownership

Lists incomplete albums, duplicates and missing releases by artists you follow. Sync Discogs and your records join the same catalogue.

How it works

Album pages

Everything about one album

Release facts, the edition you own, critic scores and your own rating and listens, on one page.

  • Scores from MusicBrainz, Discogs and Wikipedia
  • Your rating, review and listens stay yours
  • All editions of the release, one tab away
Tag plans

Preview every change

A plan shows each file and field, its value now and the value it would get.

  • Nothing is written until you apply
  • Audio is hashed before and after each write
  • Any plan can be reverted from the journal
Compilations

Rejoin split compilations

A compilation filed one folder per artist becomes one album again, without moving a file.

  • Rescans keep it together
  • Split it back at any time
  • Album fields are set in one previewed plan
Library care

See what is missing

Once albums are identified, tagave lists missing tracks, duplicates and releases you do not have yet.

  • Incomplete albums name the missing tracks
  • A dismissed gap stays dismissed
  • Unsure matches wait in a review queue

Install

One command sets everything up in ~/tagave. Running it again is safe.

$ bash -c "$(curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/xooxoxxo/tagave/main/install-tagave.sh)"

Docker Compose 2.20 or later, on Linux or macOS.

  1. Run the installer

    Tell it where your music lives.

  2. Open tagave

    Go to http://localhost:3100 and create your account.

  3. Add your music

    Enter /mnt/music as the scan root.

Questions

What is tagave?

A catalogue for music you own. It identifies each album, shows what is incomplete or duplicated, and corrects tags through plans you preview.

Is it a music player?

No. Navidrome, Jellyfin, Plexamp and Roon play your music. tagave gets the tags under them right.

Will it change my files?

Only their tags, and only when you apply a plan you have previewed. Each write is journalled and can be reverted. The audio is hashed before and after, and a mismatch stops the job.

Files are never moved, renamed, split, converted or deleted. You can also mount your music read-only.

Does it need the cloud or an account?

No. It runs in Docker on your own machine, and your library and account stay there.

It looks things up on MusicBrainz, Cover Art Archive, Wikipedia and Wikidata, plus Discogs and AcoustID if you add keys. Twice a day it checks GitHub for new tagave releases, sending nothing about you; turn that off in Settings › Updates. Nothing else leaves your server, and there is no telemetry.

What does it cost?

Nothing. It is open source under the GNU AGPL-3.0. The tag writer runs mutagen as a separate process.

Which formats does it handle?

It reads and writes tags in MP3, FLAC, M4A and ALAC, Ogg, Opus, WAV and AIFF. APE and WavPack are read only for now, and albums from a CUE sheet get album-level fields only.
